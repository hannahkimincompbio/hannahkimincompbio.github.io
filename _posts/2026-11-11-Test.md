### What's on your mind?

This post is meant to be a test, but since it is public, I guess I can write a little about it. 

When I was collecting data for the paper that was rejected, I was not developing a game full-time, because I was also looking for postdoctoral positions. And it is said that job searching is a full-time position, especially these days. As a result, I admit that that I have let my game development ideas gather dust. However, if there is one thing that I have noticed about the core mission of the game design community during summer (in hindsight) and since hearing about the submission result: game design communities expect a game designed to be tested when one makes a community appearance. 

What I currently have is very much a work-in-progress and proof-of-concept; an incomplete project. The perspectives on what should be allowed or preserved for game development vary widely, so I had a lot of hesitations. Nevertheless, I have gotten the valuable feedback from the community, so I feel that it is best that I try to address it, before the paper loses all its life. I was told that having some kind of deadlines in game development helps.
